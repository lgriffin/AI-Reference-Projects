## What this week adds

Rung 6 is a copy of rung 5 with nothing taken away. It adds fifteen requirements, R-022 to R-036, and the
code that keeps them:

- **content safety**: a URL allow-list (`safeUrl`) behind rung 2's escaping, and a Content Security Policy
  in the static site and in the reader;
- **containment**: the generator reads only inside the course folder, whatever a path or a link says;
- **identity and access**: `packages/identity` (a port) and `packages/rbac` (a pure policy that fails
  closed); a private course is built only on purpose;
- **supply chain**: a lockfile, registry sources only, and audit allowances that expire.

It adds no dependency. This is rung 6's only week, so it ends with the assignment (A6), the exit criteria
on the topic page, and a comparison with rung 6's standard version.

## The value it delivers

Rung 6 has three customers. The **student** can open any course without it costing them their session.
The **lecturer** can build a course a colleague sent without it reading their private files, and cannot
publish a private course by accident. The **contributor** gets failures that name the attack and the fix.
In the value stream from week 1, rung 6 guards every hand-over: folder to generator, generator to page,
page to browser, registry to build.
