# Kaizen (Evaluate)

## Name one waste

This week's waste is the **defect that escapes**. Candidates:

- **An escape you made.** The control-character address in step 4, green gate and all. How long would it
  have lived in a real course platform, and who would have found it?
- **An escape upstream recorded.** Fourteen tables with Row-Level Security off; a `SECURITY.md` that claims
  a header no app sends. What did each cost, or what could it have cost?
- **Relearning.** An attack someone had to rediscover because it was not in a corpus.
- **Waiting.** `pnpm audit:online` is outside the gate. When should it run, and who reads the result?

## Find its root cause

Use your Five Whys from step 8. Check that the last why is about the way of working.

## Mistake-proof it

Poka-yoke asks: could this mistake be made **impossible**, not just detected? For your waste, propose one
of:

- a required argument, or a type, that makes the wrong call fail to compile (as `access` does);
- a check moved earlier, to the moment the mistake is made (a corpus row, an architecture rule with a
  teaching message);
- a wall behind the wall, enforced by something you do not control (as the browser enforces the CSP).

Say which Assertion Ladder level your proposal reaches, and what it costs every day. Not every rule should
be L5: write one rule from this week that you would deliberately leave at L2 or L3, and why.
