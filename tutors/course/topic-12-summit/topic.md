# Week 12: The Summit

Summit, above rung 7 of 7: tutors-mono-repo

The last week. You have climbed seven rungs on one straight line, and rung 7 is a small Tutors that is
specified, tested, guarded and shipped. This week you step off the ladder onto the real thing:
[tutors-sdk/tutors-mono-repo](https://github.com/tutors-sdk/tutors-mono-repo). You map every rung to its home
upstream, meet what the real repository adds, and make one small improvement to it with a coding agent, under
the ladder: specified first, fenced by a session manifest, proved by the repository's own gates, and argued
for on one page.

## Where we are on the ladder

| Rung | Variant | This week |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 Seed to 6 Guarded | `rung-1-seed` to `rung-6-guarded` | weeks 1 to 10, done |
| 7 Shipped | `variants/rung-7-shipped` | week 11, done: its standard version is this week's starting point |
| **Summit** | tutors-mono-repo | **you are here (week 12)**: from rung 7 to the real repository, and a first contribution |

There is no new variant this week, and no Rung Fence: the Summit has none. You bring your own.

## Learning outcomes

By the end of this week you will be able to:

1. **(Analyse)** Map each rung of the ladder to the upstream areas it mirrors, and classify what
   tutors-mono-repo adds beyond rung 7 (module outcomes M1, M4).
2. **(Evaluate)** Judge at which Assertion Ladder level (L0 to L5) upstream keeps a given rule, citing the file
   that keeps it, and decide whether the rule should climb (M5, M7).
3. **(Evaluate)** Choose a small improvement by the value it adds, judge whether it sits on a rung you have
   finished, and find its root cause (M8).
4. **(Create)** Specify the improvement first, as an EARS Rule with a scenario in upstream's own style, and
   fence your agent with a session manifest (M2, M6).
5. **(Create)** Implement the improvement, run the repository's gates, and defend it on a one-page A3 and in a
   pull request (M3, M8).

The week's dominant Bloom levels are Evaluate and Create.

## Lean focus: Kaizen upstream

Every lab so far has ended with a Kaizen step: one waste, its root cause, one improvement to the standard
work. This week the Kaizen is the whole lab, and the standard work you improve is not yours: it belongs to
the Tutors maintainers. One small improvement, pulled by a real need, contributed to tutors-mono-repo. The
reading note looks back over the module and names the waste each rung removed.
