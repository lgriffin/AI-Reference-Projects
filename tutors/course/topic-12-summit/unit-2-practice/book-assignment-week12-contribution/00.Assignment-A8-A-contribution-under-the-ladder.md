# A contribution under the ladder

The capstone, assignment A8. Make one small, real improvement to tutors-mono-repo, and defend it.

You pick the improvement. You specify it first, in upstream's own style. You fence your agent with a session
manifest, implement the change, run the repository's gates, and write a one-page A3 that argues the change
was worth making. The lab, "Your first contribution", walks you through the method once; this assignment asks
you to do it on your own choice and to judge your own work.

It is assessed at Bloom's **Evaluate** and **Create** levels, against all eight module outcomes (M1 to M8).
Most of the marks are for judgement and evidence, not for the size of the diff. A one-line fix that is well
chosen, specified, proved and argued can earn full marks. A large feature without them cannot.

## Learning outcomes assessed

| Week 12 outcome | Level | Module outcomes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Map each rung to the upstream areas it mirrors, and classify what upstream adds | Analyse | M1, M4 |
| 2. Judge at which Assertion Ladder level upstream keeps a rule, and whether it should climb | Evaluate | M5, M7 |
| 3. Choose a small improvement by its value, at a rung you have finished, and find its root cause | Evaluate | M8 |
| 4. Specify it first as an EARS Rule and scenario in upstream's style, and fence your agent | Create | M2, M6 |
| 5. Implement it, run the gates, and defend it on an A3 and in a pull request | Create | M3, M8 |

You may, and are expected to, work with a coding agent. Every line it writes is yours to explain.
