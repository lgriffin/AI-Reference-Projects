# Marking rubric

The rows are the module's learning outcomes from `course.md`, each with its Bloom level. The columns are the
grade bands. Each row carries equal weight, 12.5%. The capstone as a whole is assessed at Evaluate and
Create, so every row is read at those levels: not "did you do it" but "did you choose well, and can you show
it".

| Outcome (level) | Evidence | Excellent (70 and above) | Good (60 to 69) | Pass (40 to 59) | Not yet (below 40) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **M1** Explain the course format and its data model, and how the generator, validator and reader use it (Understand) | A3 current state; AC2 | Places the change exactly in the path from course folder to reader, names the upstream files on either side of it, and explains what would break downstream | Places the change in the right package and explains what that package does | Names the files changed, with a thin account of their role | Cannot say what the changed code is for |
| **M2** Work with a coding agent under the two ladders: manifest, fence, Single Gate, Human Boundary (Apply) | session manifest; `git diff --stat`; AC6, AC7, AC8, AC12 | Manifest is specific to the task; the diff sits inside "May touch" or each exception is justified; the Human Boundary was used (the Rule approved, a question asked) and it shows | Manifest complete and followed; diff inside the fence; gates run | Manifest present but generic; diff mostly inside the fence; gates run | No manifest, or a diff that roams, or a gate loosened to go green |
| **M3** Extend a codebase with a new capability, using types and tests to keep it correct (Apply) | the diff; the tests; AC5, AC8 | The smallest change that satisfies the Rule; tests would fail without it; follows the repository's existing patterns so closely that it reads as native | Correct and tested; a little more than was needed, or slightly off the local style | Works, with thin tests or noticeable extra code | Does not work, or is untested, or cannot be explained line by line |
| **M4** Analyse a variant against its upstream counterpart, and diagnose why a check fails (Analyse) | A3 root cause; lab step 2 notes; AC2, AC10 | Root cause reached by a sound Five Whys chain that ends at a changeable cause; every red gate met is diagnosed from its message; the rung-to-upstream mapping is exact | A plausible root cause with a short chain; failures diagnosed | A cause is stated but is a symptom, or ends at a person | No root cause, or failures fixed by trial and error |
| **M5** Justify where each rule belongs on the Assertion Ladder (Analyse) | A3 countermeasure; AC11 | States the level before and after with the file that keeps it, argues from the risk and the cost of the check whether it should climb, and stops at the right level | States both levels correctly with files; some argument | Names a level, without a file or a reason | Does not use the Assertion Ladder, or misplaces the rule |
| **M6** Specify behaviour with EARS and Given/When/Then, and judge a suite by closure and mutation (Evaluate) | the Rule and scenario; audit output; red and green runs; AC3, AC4, AC5 | Rule in upstream's exact style, passes the audit, states one observable response; scenario's Then would fail on the old behaviour; written and shown red before the code; pattern choice defended | Rule passes the audit and is observable; scenario sound; order of work shown | Rule has one "shall" and an id but is vague or about the code; scenario weak | No Rule, or a Rule written after the fact, or one that fails the audit |
| **M7** Evaluate safety and release readiness from evidence (Evaluate) | pull request title and template; A3 evidence; AC8, AC12 | Judges what the change could move for a student or operator, gives the right artefact hint or a reasoned "none", considers what the release harness and CI would see, and names any risk left | Correct hint or "none"; gates and CI results reported and read correctly | Gates reported without interpretation | No evidence, or a claim the evidence does not support |
| **M8** Contribute an improvement to tutors-mono-repo under the ladder, using standard work and Kaizen (Create) | the pull request; the A3 as a whole; AC1, AC9 | A well-chosen, pulled improvement with clear value; a pull request a maintainer can review in one sitting; review answered well; the A3 is honest, fits one page, and ends with a real next step | A real improvement, cleanly submitted, with a complete A3 | An improvement of marginal value, or an A3 missing a section, or a template half filled | Nothing submitted upstream or on rung 7, or work that is not the student's own |

## Reading the bands

- **Excellent** work could be merged as it stands, and its A3 would persuade a maintainer who had not seen
  the code.
- **Good** work is correct and complete, with judgement shown but not always argued.
- **Pass** work follows the method but mostly describes rather than evaluates.
- **Not yet** means a row's evidence is missing or contradicts itself. Any gate loosened to go green, or any
  line you cannot explain when asked, caps M2 and M3 at Not yet.

Size earns nothing. The walkthrough fix, done fully, can reach Excellent in every row.
