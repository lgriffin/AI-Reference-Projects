# Kaizen (Evaluate)

Ten minutes, as every week. This is the end of rung 5.

## Name one waste

Candidates from this week:

- **A defect that escaped.** In step 5 every check in the gate was green and the page was wrong. Had you
  committed and moved on, who would have found it, and when?
- **Waiting.** The swapped-key failure took 30 seconds to say "timed out". How could that test fail faster
  and say more?
- **Relearning.** How long did step 5's "find out why" take you? What single sentence, in which file,
  would have saved the next person that time?
- **Handoffs.** `pnpm test:e2e` is outside the gate, so "did you look in a browser?" is handed from the
  tool to your memory. Did you remember every time?
- **Overproduction.** Compare your reader with the standard version (step 9). Is there anything in yours
  that no requirement pulled?

## Find its root cause

Use your "why?" chain from step 5, or do a short one now. Stop when you reach something in the standard
work that you can change.

## Improve the standard work

Make or propose one change. This week has an obvious candidate, because one of its rules is kept only by
example:

- add a line to `AGENTS.md`, "Escaping and accessibility" or "Where things go": "Across a rune, compare
  values (a route, an id), never objects: `.value` is a proxy." That raises the rule from L1 to L2. The
  manifest must stay under 500 words and has 499 (`wc -w AGENTS.md`), so your line has to replace or
  shorten another: standard work that only grows stops being read;
- or raise it further: sketch an architecture rule that fails when a `.svelte` file passes `currentLo.value`
  itself, and not one of its fields, to a function from `lib/course`. What would its message say?
- or change your own standard work: "after the gate, open the page".

Record the waste, the root cause and the change in your learning log with this week's date.

Then, before week 10, re-read your Kaizen notes from weeks 6 to 9, the four weeks since tests became the
specification. Has one waste come back more than once? Week 10 is about defects that escape, and what it
takes to make them impossible.
