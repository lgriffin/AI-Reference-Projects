# The brief

Work in your `tutors-ladder/rung-7-shipped` from Lab 11 (or a fresh copy of the variant beside `fixtures`,
with `git init`, a first commit and the tag `v1.0.0`). Start with `pnpm release:value-stream` and keep the
map: it is your "before".

## What to build

1. **A requirement.** Run `pnpm test:ears:audit --next-id`. Write one EARS sentence, after R-044 (and after
   R-045 if you did the lab), that says what the release shall do when the largest page of the site is
   over the budget of its objective. Name "the largest page" and "the release" in it. Say what the release
   **reports and decides**. Have a person approve it, and commit it with its Rule and scenario before any
   code.
2. **A scenario.** Add the sentence as a `Rule:` in `tests/bdd/features/release/release.feature`, tagged
   with its id and pattern, with at least one scenario: a build whose largest page is over a budget. Write
   its steps in `tests/bdd/steps/release/release.steps.ts`. See it fail.
3. **A measure.** Add `largest-page-bytes` to `MEASURES` in `scripts/lib/slo.ts`. It reads the size of the
   largest `.html` file the build wrote. Not `tutors.json`, not `version.json`: those are not pages a
   learner opens. A build with no page measures 0. Its advice, when the budget is exceeded, names the
   largest pages, largest first, so the reader knows where to look.
4. **An objective.** Add an objective to `release/slo.yaml` with `measure: largest-page-bytes`, an `id`, a
   `budget` in bytes and a `why`. Choose the budget from a measurement: run `pnpm release:slo` first and
   leave headroom you can defend. For the fixture, 4096 bytes or less.
5. **Evidence.** Write a test of your own, in `tests/unit/release/`, that exercises `largest-page-bytes`:
   at least one case where `tutors.json` is the largest file and the measure still reports the largest
   page.
6. **A claim.** Add a claim with `id: pages-within-budget` to `release/claims.yaml`. It rests on your
   requirement (`requirements: [R-0NN]`) and its evidence is your test.

Then release it: Conventional Commits, `pnpm release:version --from v<last>`, a curated changelog line,
the checks, the value stream again (your "after"), the A3. Tag it yourself.

## What not to change

- `evaluate` in `scripts/lib/slo.ts`: the teaching message for a breach is already there; your measure's
  `advice` fills in the middle of it.
- The other objectives' budgets, the other claims, the fence, the architecture tests and the grading test.
- `scripts/lib` stays pure: your measure reads a `Measurement`, never the disk. The "Pure release logic
  rule" in `tests/architecture/release.test.ts` will tell you if it does not.

## Upstream

Upstream budgets the reader's JavaScript and CSS per route (`scripts/checks/bundle-budget.ts` in `$MONO`).
Read it before you write your advice: how does upstream tell a contributor which chunk grew? Then look at
how its `release/slo.yaml` words a `description`, and write your `why` for the person who pays when a page
grows.
