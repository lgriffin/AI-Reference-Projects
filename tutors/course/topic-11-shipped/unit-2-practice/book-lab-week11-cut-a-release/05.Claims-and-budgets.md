# Claims and budgets (Analyse, Evaluate)

Steps 6 and 7 of the SOP. A release promises things, and keeps numbers. Each must be backed by a check.

## Read the claims

~~~bash
pnpm release:claims
~~~

~~~
release/claims.yaml: 14 claims, each with evidence that exists and runs.
~~~

Open `release/claims.yaml`. Take out your threat table from week 10, step 3. For each of your four
threats, find the claim that answers it, the requirements it rests on, and one piece of evidence. Open
that test. Would it fail if the promise broke?

Then pick one claim and answer the question from the reading note: who relies on this, and which test
goes red on the day it stops being true?

## Break one: a renamed scenario

~~~bash
sed -i.bak 's/#The policy names nothing that could run/#The policy names nothing that runs/' release/claims.yaml
pnpm release:claims; echo "exit $?"
~~~

~~~
release/claims.yaml: 1 problem(s). A release may not promise what no test checks.

- Claim "no-inline-script" cites the test "The policy names nothing that runs" in tests/bdd/features/site/safe-content.feature, and no test there has that name. A test id is the exact name in test("...") or the exact title of a Scenario. Did you mean: "Every page of the shared fixture carries the policy, first", "The policy names nothing that could run"?
exit 1
~~~

The message names the rule, the offender and the fix: L4. Restore the file:

~~~bash
mv release/claims.yaml.bak release/claims.yaml
~~~

## Break one: a promise with no evidence

Add this claim at the end of `release/claims.yaml` and run the check again:

~~~yaml

  - id: fast-pages
    promise: Every page loads in under a second on a slow phone.
    evidence: []
~~~

~~~
- Claim "fast-pages" promises "Every page loads in under a second on a slow phone." but names no evidence. A promise without a test is only told (L2), not checked (L3). Add a test that would fail if the promise broke, and list it as evidence: "<file>#<test name>".
~~~

Decide: can you write a test for that promise with what rung 7 has? If not, the sentence belongs in the
changelog's prose, or nowhere. Remove it (`git checkout release/claims.yaml`).

## Break one: skipped evidence

In `tests/unit/lite/csp.test.ts`, change `test("R-023: every page carries ...` to `test.skip(` and run:

~~~bash
pnpm release:claims
~~~

~~~
- Claim "no-inline-script" cites "R-023: every page carries the policy as its first meta after the charset, and no script" in tests/unit/lite/csp.test.ts, which is skipped. A skipped test is not evidence: it never runs in the gate. Unskip it or cite one that runs.
~~~

Rung 4's suite-health check would catch the skip too. Why is it worth catching here as well, in the
release's own words? Undo it (`git checkout tests`).

## The budgets

~~~bash
pnpm release:slo
~~~

~~~
ok   build-time: the time to build the fixture site is 101 ms, within its budget of 2000 ms (95% headroom).
ok   site-size: the size of the whole site is 18.2 kB, within its budget of 24.4 kB (25% headroom).
ok   reader-size: the size of the built reader is 122.8 kB, within its budget of 156.3 kB (21% headroom).
~~~

Your time differs. The script builds the reader first, then builds the fixture three times with the real
command and keeps the slowest time: a budget should hold on a bad day.

## Exceed a budget

~~~bash
sed -i.bak 's/budget: 25000/budget: 16000/' release/slo.yaml
pnpm release:slo; echo "exit $?"
~~~

~~~
FAIL SLO "site-size" exceeded: the size of the whole site is 18.2 kB, over its budget of 15.6 kB by 2.6 kB (17%). The largest files are tutors.json (5.1 kB), topic-01-basics/index.html (1.9 kB), index.html (1.7 kB). Shrink what is not value to a learner (repeated markup, unused styles). If the growth is value a learner gets, raise the budget in release/slo.yaml in its own pull request that says why. Never raise a budget to make a release pass: a budget is a ratchet, as upstream's slo/floors.yaml in the release harness is.
exit 1
~~~

~~~bash
mv release/slo.yaml.bak release/slo.yaml
~~~

Find the four parts of that message: by how much, where to look, what to do, what never to do.

## Evaluate: where each budget is judged

Open `tests/release/budgets.test.ts`. It judges the size of the site inside `pnpm test`, but not the build
time and not the reader's size. Read its header comment. Then judge:

- What would happen to a team whose gate failed one run in twenty because a laptop was busy?
- `reader-size` has 21% headroom. Is the budget too loose, too tight, or right? What evidence would change
  your mind?
- Open `$MONO/release/slo.yaml`. What does `reader-availability` promise, and over what window? Why can
  rung 7 not have that objective?
