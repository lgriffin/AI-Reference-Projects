# Kaizen (Evaluate)

## Name one waste

Use your value stream map from step 7. This week's wastes have numbers on them. Candidates:

- **Waiting.** The sixteen hours before the Reviewer read the A3, or your own equivalent. Nobody chose it.
- **Overprocessing.** The reader built three times in one release. Mutation testing run in full when one
  file changed.
- **Defects.** `pnpm release:version --from v1.0.0 --write`, run twice, once released the same commits as
  1.2.0. Walking this very lab found it (`RUNG.md`, "Mutation testing"). How long could that have lived?
- **Relearning.** A commit called "Update notes". What did the next person have to rediscover?
- **Handoffs.** Captain to Contributor to Reviewer. What was lost at each?

Pick one, and write its cost as a number from your map: seconds, hours, or releases.

## Find its root cause

Ask why five times. Check that the last why is about the way of working, not about a person. "The reviewer
was slow" is not a cause. "Nobody is told when an A3 is ready" is.

## Propose one countermeasure

Choose the smallest change that would move your number, and say which kind it is:

- a step removed, merged or reordered in `release/SOP.md` (build the reader once, and say which step owns it);
- a check moved earlier, to the moment the mistake is made (a commit-message check when the commit is
  written, not a warning at release time);
- a wait made visible (the A3 posted where the Reviewer already looks);
- a rule raised a level on the Assertion Ladder, with its daily cost stated.

Then predict: by how much should the lead time or the process time fall? A countermeasure without a
predicted number cannot be checked next release.

## Try it, and measure again

If your countermeasure fits in half an hour, make it, in its own commit, and run
`pnpm release:value-stream` again. Compare the two maps. Did the number move as predicted? If it did not,
that is a finding, not a failure: write down what you learned.

Not every number should be driven down. Name one step whose time you would deliberately **not** cut, and
why. (What does the mutation run buy? What does the Reviewer's reading buy?)

## Improve the standard work

`release/SOP.md` ends with "Improving this sheet": change one thing, in its own commit, and measure the
next release against this one. Propose your one change to the sheet, or a sharper wording for one line of
`AGENTS.md`, from what you measured this week. Record it in your learning log with its before and after
numbers.
