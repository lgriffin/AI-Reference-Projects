# Formalise your rules from weeks 1 to 5 (Evaluate, Create)

Take out the "Name the rule" sentences you wrote in weeks 1 to 5: the ones given in each lab, and your own.
The reading note works through the given ones; now do yours.

## Sort them

For each sentence, decide first which kind of rule it is:

| Kind | Example | Where it lives |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Product behaviour** a lecturer or student could observe | week 3: "The site builder shall escape every piece of course text ..." | `REQUIREMENTS.md`, as EARS, proved by scenarios |
| **A rule about the code** | week 4: "The workspace shall let each package import only ..." | `tests/architecture/`, an executable rule with a teaching failure |
| **Already here** | week 1: "... the generator shall leave the folder ... out of `tutors.json`" | find its id (R-001 and its scenario "A folder that names no kind is not content") |

## Rewrite the product ones

For each product rule that rung 4 does not already hold, write a row: id (from `--next-id`), pattern, one
"shall", one of rung 4's system names, an observable response, a layer. Critique it against step 5's list,
then pass it through the audit.

## Test-first, enforced

Add your best one to `REQUIREMENTS.md` and run the gate:

~~~bash
pnpm test 2>&1 | grep -E "unproved|FAIL"
~~~

~~~
 FAIL  tests/suite-health/requirements.test.ts > this repository: every requirement is proved, and every citation is known
unproved-requirement: REQUIREMENTS.md:24 R-013: no Rule tagged @R-013 has a scenario. Add one to the feature for its layer in tests/bdd/features. ...
~~~

The gate refuses a requirement without proof. That is test-first, made mechanical: next week's labs start
here, with a scenario. Keep your row in your notes, and undo with `git checkout REQUIREMENTS.md` (next
week's lab takes `R-013` for a rule of its own).
